## 1. Executive Summary

The 2026 U.S. EV market is defined by two simultaneous forces: a near-complete transition of legacy automakers to electrified lineups, and a sharp policy reversal that strips out the federal Clean Vehicle Credit.

Key facts to anchor the year:

- **No federal EV tax credit.** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), signed July 4, 2025, repealed the $7,500 Section 30D credit, the $4,000 used-EV credit (Section 25E), and the Commercial Clean Vehicle Credit (Section 45W). The credits terminated for vehicles *not* delivered to buyers before September 30, 2025. Almost every 2026 EV transaction is therefore now full sticker price, with state credits and dealer incentives becoming the primary federal-style offsets.
- **The "lease loophole" closed.** OBBBA ended the path where leased EVs passed through the dealer/manufacturer and qualified for the prior $7,500 commercial credit. Many of the deeply discounted sub-$300/month EV leases marketed in 2024–2025 no longer exist in 2026.
- **Tariffs continue to bite.** A 100% tariff on Chinese-built EVs, 25% Section 232 tariffs on steel/aluminum, and additional Section 232/Section 301 tariffs on EV batteries and critical minerals remain in place. This effectively forecloses any direct Chinese-brand EV imports and raises the bill of materials on every American-built EV that uses Chinese battery cells or graphite.
- **NACS has won the connector war.** Every major automaker has either completed or committed to Tesla's North American Charging Standard (J3400), starting with Ford and GM in 2024 and ending with the last holdouts in 2025–2026. Most 2026 EVs ship with NACS ports; legacy CCS1 adapters are shipped in the glovebox for older DC fast-charge stations.
- **The 2026 lineup is the most competitive ever.** Roughly 80–90 distinct EV nameplates are available or imminent in 2026, ranging from a sub-$22,000 compact crossover from Slate to a $340,000 hand-built Cadillac Celestiq.

## 2. The Policy Environment: Why 2026 Is Different from 2025

Omitting the policy changes would render this report useless to any consumer or analyst. The 2026 buying environment is materially worse on tax incentives and tariff-driven battery costs, but stable or improved on charging and model choice.

### 2.1 The End of Federal Credits

OBBBA (Pub. L. 119-21) ended the following effective September 30, 2025:

| Credit | Prior value | Status in 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Clean Vehicle Credit (30D) — new EVs | Up to $7,500 | Repealed |
| Credit for Previously-Owned Clean Vehicles (25E) | Up to $4,000 | Repealed |
| Commercial Clean Vehicle Credit (45W) — leased EVs | Up to $7,500 commercial credit | Repealed for vehicles placed in service after Sept 30, 2025 |
| Alternative Fuel Vehicle Refueling Property Credit (30C) | 30% of charger cost | Repealed |

The credits are gone, not paused. There is no "phase out" triggered by sales milestones in 2026 because the underlying statute has been deleted.

### 2.2 What Remains

- **State credits and rebates.** California's CVRP, Colorado's tax credit, Massachusetts' MOR-EV, New York's Drive Clean, Washington's sales-tax exemption, and roughly a dozen other state programs remain intact. Some (e.g., Oregon's Charge Ahead rebate) were *expanded* in 2025 to fill the federal gap.
- **Utility rebates.** Investor-owned utilities in California, Texas, the Northeast, and the Pacific Northwest continue to offer $500–$2,500 rebates for EV purchases and home chargers.
- **Section 179D / business depreciation.** Commercial EVs can still be depreciated under standard bonus depreciation rules; this is not a federal "EV credit" but provides real tax savings for fleets.

### 2.3 Tariffs and Supply Chain Costs

- **100% Section 301 tariff on Chinese EVs** (raised from 25% in 2024) — this forecloses BYD, Geely, NIO, Xpeng, and others from the U.S. direct-import market. Even a hypothetical $15,000 Chinese EV would land in the U.S. at $30,000+ after tariff before any other markup.
- **25% Section 232 tariffs on steel and aluminum** — raises body-in-white cost.
- **Section 301 tariffs of 7.5%–25% on EV batteries, lithium-ion non-EV batteries, battery components, and critical minerals** — these hit Chinese-dominant inputs (graphite, LFP cells, cathode precursors) whether or not the vehicle is built in China.
- **Canada/Mexico USMCA tariffs** at 25% in 2025 — relief was negotiated for vehicles meeting USMCA rules of origin, so most North-American-built EVs are not directly tariffed, but parts crossing back and forth are.

The net effect: the Inflation Reduction Act's intent to localize the EV supply chain partially materialized; automakers accelerated U.S. battery plants in 2023–2025 partly to qualify for the now-defunct credit. Those investments continue to operate and continue to matter for IRA Section 45X (which provides per-unit manufacturing credits, not consumer credits, and was *not* repealed).
